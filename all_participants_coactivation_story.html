Coactivation Across All Participants

40 sessions, 21 participants, across the three main playlists ("Life Sounds", "Life Sounds new", "The Tree of Life"; test/demo sessions excluded). All names have been replaced with anonymous codes (P1–P21), assigned by each participant's earliest session date.

1. What "coactivation" means here

Coactivation is co-stimulation of the sympathetic and parasympathetic branches at the same time — the specific autonomic-flexibility signature this practice is meant to train, distinct from ordinary stress peaks (SNS up, PNS down) or ordinary recovery (PNS up, SNS down). Detection follows a z-score method against each participant's own resting baseline:

  • RMSSD is log-transformed (ln RMSSD) before any statistics are computed on it.
  • Each participant's Individual Baseline Range — personal mean/SD for HR and ln(RMSSD) — is pooled across all of that participant's own pre-session resting windows, split into 60-second sub-windows.
  • Every 60-second window in a session is then converted to a Z-score against that personal baseline. A window qualifies when Z(HR) ≥ +1.0 AND Z(ln RMSSD) ≥ 0.0.
  • Persistence filter: a single qualifying window on its own does not count. Only runs of 2 or more consecutive qualifying windows count as coactivation — i.e. it must last more than 1 minute to be counted at all. This removes single-minute blips and is applied throughout everything below.

Baseline reliability varies by participant: most contribute a single pre-session baseline (∼5 pooled 60s sub-windows), so their personal mean/SD is a noisier estimate than a participant with several sessions. Treat single-session coactivation numbers as indicative, not precise.

2. Coactivation by participant

With the >1-minute persistence filter applied, 3 of 19 participants (with usable data) show any qualifying coactivation across their sessions — down from 12 of 19 before the filter, since most of what was counted before was single-minute blips. Bars are colored by each participant's own baseline RMSSD tertile (data-driven Low/Medium/High, relative to the other participants in this dataset — ■ Low, ■ Medium, ■ High), with session count (n) labeled per bar.

The 3 participants with any qualifying coactivation are P1 (14.0 min, Medium tertile), P13 (7.0 min, High tertile), and P11 (2.0 min, High tertile). With only 3 nonzero participants, this is far too few to read a tertile pattern into — two of three happen to be in the High tertile, but that could easily be chance at this sample size.

4. ΔSD2, post − pre, by playlist

SD2 (Poincaré long-term variability) is this project's primary autonomic-flexibility metric that stays valid regardless of in-session breathing pattern, unlike RMSSD. Each point is one session's change, post-session resting window minus pre-session resting window; boxes group sessions by playlist:

Of the 39 sessions with both a pre and post window, SD2 rose in 18 and fell in 21 — roughly an even split. By playlist, median ΔSD2 was near zero for Life Sounds (+0.2ms, n=14), positive for Life Sounds new (+5.4ms, n=12), and negative for The Tree of Life (−2.2ms, n=13) — a suggestive but not dispositive playlist difference given the small per-group samples.

Caveats

• N=21 participants, most with a single session — playlist- and participant-level comparisons above are provisional, especially for "Life Sounds new" (12 sessions) and "The Tree of Life" (12 sessions).
• Baseline precision varies by participant — a participant with one session has their entire personal Z-score reference built from ∼5 pooled 60-second sub-windows, versus dozens for a participant with many sessions.
• At least one display name in the source data maps to two different underlying accounts. Anonymizing by name merges those sessions under a single code here; their baselines were computed separately per account, so treat that participant's numbers as a merged approximation.
• This is exploratory analysis on a proof-of-concept dataset, not a validated clinical or research instrument.
