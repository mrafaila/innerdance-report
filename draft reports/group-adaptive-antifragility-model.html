A Group- and Level-Adaptive Model for Training Coactivation via Sound

Compiles the antifragility-coupled extension to the coactivation causal model: two participant groups, six response-shape classes, the parameters and control law linking them, the closed-loop causal model, the proposed experiment, and the self-report proxy design. No new claims beyond what was already derived — this is the compact, unambiguous reference version.

What this study would show, and how the theory was reached

Why antifragility, why anxiety

Anxiety is characterized in the literature as autonomic inflexibility, not just "too much arousal": reduced HF-HRV and vagal tone are consistently found across anxiety disorders (Hedges' g ≈ −0.29 to −0.39 across two independent meta-analyses — Chalmers et al. 2014; Cheng et al. 2022), and Friedman's (2007) autonomic flexibility–neurovisceral integration model frames anxiety specifically as a restricted response range — a system too rigid to flexibly engage and disengage its own vagal brake. Antifragility theory's claim is that a system exposed to graded, recoverable perturbation becomes progressively more flexible, not just more tolerant. If anxiety is low flexibility, and antifragility training raises flexibility, a controlled dose-then-recovery sound arc is a plausible lever on the exact mechanism anxiety is defined by — which is also why Group H (overstressed, rarely-coactivating participants, defined in §1) are the antifragility-relevant candidates, not a side group.

The chain of reasoning, in order:

  • Coactivation (Weissman & Mendes 2021) — sympathetic and parasympathetic activity elevated together, not reciprocally — gave a precise, testable mechanism for why a controlled-stress-then-recovery sound arc might train the ANS.
  • The 12-stage design already has this exact shape: a dose (Stage 3, Threshold — the only stage whose chemistry names cortisol) followed by recovery (Stages 10–12) — but closer reading of the stage chemistry/circuitry shows three such dose→recovery cycles in one session, not one (the "3 waves").
  • Antifragility (Axenie et al. 2023) sharpens the claim: a trained system shouldn't just recover, its post-perturbation baseline should measurably exceed the pre-perturbation one, repeated over trials/sessions.
  • Applying this to real participants surfaces a confound: not everyone starts at the same place. Some run too hot during the dose and rarely reach coactivation at all; others reach it easily and may need less stimulus to keep doing so. This is the two-group theory this document formalizes.

The antifragility pattern, visually

Schematic redrawing of the response pattern Axenie et al. (2023) describe for a system hit with repeated perturbations over time — not the paper's own figure, but the same described shape: fragile degrades further each time, robust plateaus at a reduced level, resilient returns to exactly where it started, antifragile rises above its own previous baseline after each hit.

fragile robust (plateaus below baseline) resilient (returns to baseline) antifragile (rises above previous baseline each time)

What the study would show: that per-person, per-group adaptive dosing — graded up for the overstressed group, tapered down for the already-coactivating group — increases coactivation frequency over sessions for the first group and preserves it at lower input for the second. That is the actual test of whether the sound stimulus trains ANS flexibility, as opposed to merely evoking it once per session.

Flexibility vs. coactivation — not the same kind of thing

CoactivationFlexibility
What it isan event: did both branches spike together, right now, at this stagea capacity: how much of this person's total heartbeat variability is active, reversible vagal control
When it can be measuredonly during/just after a stimulus (needs something to react to)any time, including at rest with no stimulus at all — it's a property of the resting system too
How it's scoredpass/fail per stage: coactivation_index > τ_coact, yes or noa continuous ratio (SD1/SD2) with no fixed threshold — read as a trend going up or down, not a yes/no
Relationship between the twoflexibility is the hypothesized precondition: a person with higher flexibility is expected to reach coactivation more easily at a given dose. Coactivation frequency (CR) is the session-level readout used to check whether flexibility is actually improving — flexibility is the thing being trained, coactivation events are the evidence it's working

Flexibility — definition and how it's measured

flexibility_index = SD1/SD2 (Poincaré short-axis over long-axis), not raw SD2: SD1 is the fast, vagally-mediated component (≈RMSSD/√2 — Porges' "vagal brake"), while SD2 is total, mixed-source variability. The ratio asks what fraction of total variability is active, reversible, vagal control. Self-report side is not yet fielded — two candidate proxies, both still to be designed into the study:

  • the Brief Resilience Scale (a 6-item trait questionnaire, Smith et al. 2008) given periodically across sessions, as a self-report trend to compare against the across-session flexibility trend
  • the variance of a continuous in-session dial reading within a single stage — how much someone's self-rated state moves around, as a self-report analogue of the within-session trend

Tracked as a trend in two places: within one session — does the ratio rise at each successive recovery trough (class E, below); and across sessions — does the resting/baseline ratio rise over the protocol (the cross-session antifragility claim).

Control law — one adaptive staircase per participant

GroupRule
Hd ← d − Δ if OR > OR_thresh; d ← d + Δ/2 only once OR ≤ OR_thresh and CR improved vs. last session
Ld ← d − Δ as long as CR ≥ CR_thresh (taper to minimal effective dose); step back up by Δ the moment CR drops below threshold

Self-report proxy (fast, two anchor items, peak stages only)

  • Item 1 (coactivation anchor, existing): "activated and relaxed at once", 1–5 → CR_self = fraction of peak stages with item 1 ≥ threshold
  • Item 2 (overstress anchor): "more than I could handle right now", 1–5 → OR_self = fraction with item 2 ≥ threshold and item 1 < threshold
  • Adaptive branch: a 3rd clarifying prompt fires only when items 1+2 disagree with the live HRV read — otherwise stop at 2 items
  • One optional open-text box, session end only: "Anything these numbers missed?" — the falsification valve

Extends the open-loop model in causal-model-coactivation.md with a closed feedback loop — the group/dose controller is the concrete form of antifragility's Path 3 ("nonlinear control synthesis / learning of optimal driving signals").

Experiment workflow

Session 0 — standalone stress test, no playlist → calibrate HR_ceil, A_ceil, sensitivity floor (per participant) → classify: Group H / Group L / unclassified Sessions 1..N — playlist, dose/structure set by group Group H: unlock waves progressively (Wave1 → +Wave2 → +Wave3), advance a wave only after OR stays below threshold 2 sessions running Group L: taper dose via duration/repeats within a wave (not just amplitude), seeking the minimal effective dose every ~3 sessions → re-run the Session-0 stress test alone (dose-independent readout of whether trained capacity is actually changing) Stop: Group H → CR clears threshold 2 consecutive sessions, OR a declared session cap is hit Group L → CR drops below threshold once while tapering → step back up one level, hold
Duration/session cap (e.g. 8–12 sessions) is a provisional design choice per Charter §2 Budget Discipline — not derived from vault evidence, and must be reported as a cap hit, not silently extended.

Where this lands: a cascaded set of models, not one model

The ~100-session / ~50-participant dataset gets clustered — by (CR, OR) peak-window signature and by recovery-shape class (A–F) — into subdatasets, and the plausibility checks in §2 are fit and validated separately within each cluster, rather than once over the pooled data:

  • Model_H — fit only on sessions/participants labeled Group H: tests whether the dose-ramp control law actually raises CR over sessions
  • Model_L — fit only on Group L: tests whether d can be tapered while CR holds
  • Within each, a further split by shape class (A–F) where sample size allows — e.g. does Group H further split into "responds to wave-unlocking" vs. "stays class D regardless"
  • The pooled, unclustered model (the original open-loop causal model) remains the baseline these cascaded models are compared against — if clustering adds no predictive power over the pooled model, that's itself a finding (check #10), not a failure to report quietly

Assumptions carried into this model, unverified

  • HR as a sympathetic-activity proxy. The original coactivation construct (Weissman & Mendes) uses PEP (pre-ejection period) as the sympathetic marker — not measurable with a Polar H10. HR is used instead, a cruder substitute that reflects both branches, not a clean sympathetic readout.
  • SD1/RMSSD as the vagal/parasympathetic proxy — standard in the HRV literature, but still an inference from Poincaré/beat-interval geometry, not a direct vagal-tone measurement.
  • SD1/SD2 as "flexibility" — a theoretical mapping (vagal time-constant argument, Porges' vagal brake), not itself a validated named metric under this exact ratio in the cited literature.
  • Acoustic-feature-to-local-column mapping (Section 1 of causal-model-coactivation.md) is a naming-similarity guess, not confirmed against the actual data dictionary.
  • 3-wave segmentation of the 12 stages is read off the design map's chemistry/circuitry language as a proxy for intended arousal — not yet checked against real per-stage HR.
  • All thresholds (HR_ceil, A_ceil, τ_coact, CR_thresh, OR_thresh, step size Δ) are placeholders to be fit from data, not pre-calibrated values.
  • Group↔shape coupling (H→D/B shapes, L→A/F-efficient shapes) and the two-group separability itself are both assumed, not confirmed — checks #9–10 above exist specifically to test them.
  • Within-session "ratchets up" (class E) extends antifragility's own cross-trial claim to within one continuous session — the paper itself doesn't make this claim at this granularity.
  • Session cap / protocol duration is a provisional design choice, not evidence-based.
  • None of the adaptive-dosing machinery in §2–3 has been run against the real dataset — the existing ~100 sessions are fixed-dose and mostly cross-sectional, not collected under any adaptive protocol.
