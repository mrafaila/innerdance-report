04 · Coactivation acoustic score

Coactivation-target samples do not cleanly cluster toward the high end

coactivation_acoustic_score = min(drive, smoothness) — never an average, so one weak side can't be hidden by a strong one. drive combines tempo, onset density, low-frequency energy ratio, and loudness; smoothness combines inverse dissonance and inverse spectral flatness.

Empirical result: Coactivation-target mean score 0.466 (n=16) vs. Activation/Calm-priority mean 0.526 (n=15). Coactivation-target samples are mixed in with — and on average slightly below — the Activation/Calm-priority samples on this acoustic score, not clustered above them.

05 · Methodology & limitations

What these numbers are, and aren't

  • Dissonance proxy. dissonance_proxy is 1 minus mean per-frame chroma tonal clarity — a tonal-concentration proxy, not a validated psychoacoustic roughness/dissonance model.
  • Dynamic range. dynamic_range_db is a peak/RMS crest factor, not EBU R128 loudness range (LRA); pyloudnorm gives integrated LUFS but not LRA directly.
  • AM modulation rate always returns a number. It's the dominant peak of the RMS envelope's power spectrum in 0.5–40 Hz — a track with no real periodic pulsing still returns some peak frequency. Use am_modulation_salience (peak/median power ratio) in feature_table.csv to judge whether a given peak is a real pulse or spectral noise before trusting Section 03's band-match table at the individual-sample level.
  • Bass/sub-bass energy is absolute, not loudness-normalized. A louder track reads higher on both bands even at the same spectral balance. The heatmap's per-feature min-max normalization corrects for this across the sample set; raw values in feature_table.csv should not be compared across tracks without accounting for lufs.
  • 4 of 31 file matches are inferred, not verified (Section 01) — treat 1.3, 7.1, 8.1, and 10.1 with more caution than the other 27 samples.
