HRV Standards, 1996 — Key Metrics for Innerdance

The Task Force of the European Society of Cardiology & NASPE (1996) is the foundational reference that defines the standard HRV frequency bands, the core time-domain measures, and introduces the Poincaré plot as a geometric method. This page pulls out only the metrics this project's pipeline actually uses, in plain definitions, visually.

Source: Task Force of ESC & NASPE (1996). Heart rate variability: standards of measurement, physiological interpretation and clinical use. Circulation, 93(5):1043–1065. · DOI: 10.1161/01.CIR.93.5.1043

1. The frequency spectrum (log scale)

VLF0.003–0.04 Hz

HF0.15–0.40 Hz

Bar widths are log-scaled (each band spans a different order of magnitude, not an equal slice of Hz). Total power is everything ≤0.40 Hz. These exact bands are what edgardo_frequency_domain_analysis.ipynb and this project's PSD code use.

2. Time domain — beat-to-beat variability

RMSSD
Root Mean Square of Successive Differences
RMSSD = sqrt( mean( (RR[i+1] − RR[i])² ) )
Reflects: short-term, beat-to-beat variability — dominated by vagal (parasympathetic) tone. The most vagally-specific time-domain measure in the paper.
Our use: pre/post 5-min resting windows ONLY — a slow/paced breathing rate inflates it artifactually, so it never appears as an in-session metric.
SDNN
Standard Deviation of NN intervals
SDNN = std( RR[1..n] )
Reflects: total variability — every cyclic component in the recording, both branches combined, not just the HF band.
Our use: breathing-robust summary for in-session windows, where breathing rate is unknown and RMSSD can't be trusted.

3. Frequency domain — spectral power

VLF power
Very Low Frequency, 0.003–0.04 Hz
Reflects: the least well-characterized band — linked to thermoregulation, the renin-angiotensin system, and slow physiological rhythms rather than a clean SNS/PNS signal. The paper itself is cautious about interpreting it in short recordings.
Our finding: VLF is the most outlier-prone band in a 5-min window — a single sensor-contact dropout session produced a ~1000× spike, forcing a log-scale display.
LF power
Low Frequency, 0.04–0.15 Hz
Reflects: a mix of sympathetic and parasympathetic activity plus baroreflex modulation — never a "pure" sympathetic marker, contrary to older shorthand.
Our use: tracked per-session as one input to LF/HF, never read alone as "sympathetic tone."
HF power
High Frequency, 0.15–0.40 Hz
Reflects: respiratory sinus arrhythmia (RSA) — vagally mediated, synchronized with the breathing cycle. The cleanest parasympathetic spectral marker.
Our use: valid in-session even when breathing rate is unknown or slow — CLAUDE.md's original in-session recommendation, alongside RSA amplitude.
LF/HF ratio
Low/High Frequency ratio
LF/HF = LF power ÷ HF power
Reflects: proposed as a "sympathovagal balance" index — but the 1996 paper itself already flags this interpretation as debated, since LF is not sympathetically pure.
Our use: reported descriptively per session/stage, never as a standalone balance verdict.

4. Geometric method — the Poincaré plot

The 1996 paper introduces the Poincaré plot (RR[i] vs. RR[i+1]) as a geometric alternative to time/frequency methods; SD1/SD2 as its two numeric axes were formalized in follow-up work, not in this paper's own tables — included here because they are core to this project's pipeline.

SD1
Poincaré short-term (minor) axis
SD1 = RMSSD / √2
Reflects: mathematically redundant with RMSSD — same vagal signal, same breathing-rate confound.
Our use: kept for Poincaré-plot convention only; never reported alongside RMSSD in the same table (double-counting the same measurement).
SD2
Poincaré long-term (major) axis
SD2 = sqrt( 2·SDNN² − 0.5·mean(diff(RR)²) )
Reflects: long-term variability across both branches — closely correlated with SDNN in short windows.
Our use: the primary in-session/cross-stage autonomic-flexibility metric — valid at any breathing rate, drives the Stage 4→10 "resilience arc."
SD1/SD2
Poincaré ratio
Reflects: a sympathovagal-balance proxy, shaped like LF/HF but from the time domain.
Our use: descriptive only, in-session — its numerator (SD1) shares RMSSD's breathing-rate sensitivity, so it's not used as a primary in-session metric.
⚠ The one rule that matters most: RMSSD and SD1 (and by extension SD1/SD2) are only physiologically valid in the pre/post 5-minute resting windows, where breathing is uncontrolled but at least not the slow, entrained breathing that innerdance sessions can induce. In-session, use SD2, SDNN, and HF power — none of them are confounded by breathing rate. This is the same rule already documented in CLAUDE.md and hrv_core.py; this page just traces it back to where the metrics themselves are defined.
